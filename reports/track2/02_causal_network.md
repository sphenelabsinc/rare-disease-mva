# Stage 2: Evidence-annotated causal network

Generated by `scripts/track2/s2_network.py` from the curated edges in
`config/track2/causal_edges.tsv`. Every curated edge has a PMID, a tier and a
status. The network adds STRING (40 partners at score ≥0.7) and Reactome
(10 pathways) as context.

Outputs:

- `tables/s2_network_edges.tsv`: the machine-readable graph, using this
  schema: `source, target, relation, module, status, tier, pmids, note,
  origin`.
- `s2_network.mmd`: a Mermaid diagram. Solid arrows are demonstrated, dashed
  are inferred, dotted are speculative.
- `tables/s2_string_partners.tsv` and `tables/s2_reactome_pathways.tsv`.

There are 35 curated edges: 27 demonstrated, 6 inferred and 2 speculative.

## Causal hierarchy as curated

**Primary defect** (where restoring function is possible):

```
p.Leu737*  --(NMD, inferred)-->  no allele-1 protein
p.Asn1002Lys --(destabilises fold, inferred from analogous alleles)--> BUBR1 fold stability
HSP90 --(chaperones)--> fold stability --> BUBR1 abundance <-- (degrades) proteasome
NAD+ --> SIRT2 --(deacetylates K668)--> BUBR1 abundance <-- (acetylates K668) CBP
BUBR1 abundance --(dosage-dependent)--> SAC strength --> missegregation, PCS --> MVA
pseudokinase domain --> KARD phosphorylation (with PLK1) --> PP2A-B56 --> stable attachments
p.Asn1002Lys ..(speculative, M3)..> pseudokinase-domain function
```

**Secondary responses** (context-dependent):

- missegregation → micronuclei → cGAS-STING. The direction of the effect on
  MVA tissue is unknown.
- MVA → p53/p21 → senescence. p53 is *net protective* in BubR1 progeroid
  tissue (Baker 2013, *Cell Rep*).
- MVA → p16Ink4a senescence → progeroid decline. Genetic clearance delays
  the decline (Baker 2011).
- MVA → apoptosis, including p53-independent apoptosis → growth failure and
  microcephaly.
- mTORC1 hyperactivity ↔ sarcopenia. This is a correlation in monoallelic
  carriers (Sieben 2020), and the link back to MVA is *speculative*.

**Clinical outcomes:**

- Embryonal tumours (RMS, Wilms): demonstrated for biallelic BUB1B/TRIP13.
- Recurrent pregnancy loss: inferred.
- Chemotherapy and radiation hypersensitivity: inferred from genomic
  instability disorders.
- Higher BUBR1 lowers tumour incidence and extends healthspan (Baker 2013,
  *Nat Cell Biol*, E3). **This is the key cancer-safety argument for Branch
  A**: raising BUBR1 protects against cancer in mice, even with oncogenic Ras.

## Reactome context

BUB1B maps to the following Reactome pathways: Mitotic Prometaphase;
Amplification of signal from unattached kinetochores via a MAD2 inhibitory
signal; Inactivation of APC/C via direct inhibition; APC/C:Cdc20-mediated
degradation of mitotic proteins (Cyclin A, Nek2A); Separation and resolution
of sister chromatid cohesion; RHO GTPases activate formins; EML4 and NUDC in
spindle formation.

## STRING context

The highest-confidence partners are the MCC/APC/C core (BUB3, MAD2L1, CDC20,
ANAPC subunits), kinetochore partners (KNL1, BUB1, CENPE, CENPF, ZWINT,
MIS12), PLK1 and PPP2CA. These are almost all essential mitotic machinery. No
approved drug *raises* their function. The drugs that touch them are
cytotoxic inhibitors, which belong on the avoid list (see Stage 3).

## What the network implies for drug search

1. The only mechanism-level levers that are demonstrated for **raising**
   BUBR1 are NAD+/SIRT2 (E3), and HSP90 chaperoning plus proteasome
   protection (E2 in vitro, analogous alleles).
2. All the levers on the mitotic machinery itself go the wrong way.
3. The downstream levers (senescence, mTORC1, inflammation) do not depend on
   the mechanism, but they treat consequences, not the defect. They also
   carry tumour-barrier trade-offs.
