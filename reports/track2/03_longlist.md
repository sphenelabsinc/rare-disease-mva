# Stage 3: Approved-drug longlist

Generated by `scripts/track2/s3_drugs.py`. The full table is
`tables/s3_longlist.tsv`, with one row per drug–target pair.

## Method

- **Targets:** 26 curated intervention targets
  (`config/track2/intervention_targets.tsv`, each with a desired direction)
  plus BUB1B's 40 STRING partners (score ≥0.7).
- **ChEMBL:** we pulled curated mechanisms for every ChEMBL target that
  contains the gene (single proteins and complexes), and kept molecules at
  max_phase 4 (approved).
- **Open Targets:** cross-checked with `drugAndClinicalCandidates` from the
  2026 Platform schema. 76 of 78 rows were confirmed.
- **Direction check:** each row is labelled with the drug's action against
  the desired direction for its target.
- All API responses are cached in `data/work/track2/cache/drugs/`, so re-runs
  are deterministic.

## What the database search cannot find (key finding)

**No approved drug has a curated mechanism on any of the Branch A
"raise BUBR1" targets.** These targets returned nothing:

- SIRT2, NAMPT, NMNAT1, CREBBP
- HSP90AA1/AB1, HSF1, BCL2L1
- cGAS/STING1, TP53, AURKB, TTK, PLK1, KIF18A
- BUB1B itself

The reasons:

1. **Substrate-type drugs are invisible to target-based search.** Niacin
   works as an NAD+ *precursor*, not by binding a target. Its ChEMBL
   mechanism is the HCAR2 receptor, which is irrelevant here. So it had to be
   added by mechanism reasoning.
2. **Drugs with network-level mechanisms are also missed.** Arimoclomol (a
   heat-shock co-inducer), phenylbutyrate (a chemical chaperone) and
   hydroxychloroquine (lysosomal/autophagy) were added for the same reason.
3. **Annotations can route through an unexpected target.** Metformin is
   annotated to mitochondrial complex I, not AMPK, and rapalogs to FKBP12,
   not mTOR. We found the second case and fixed it by adding FKBP1A.
   Everolimus and temsirolimus still didn't come through ChEMBL and were
   added by name.
4. **We searched ChEMBL for any compound with measured BUBR1 activity,**
   looking for a candidate pharmacological chaperone. There were 15
   compounds, and none is an approved drug at relevant potency:
   - dasatinib leaves >75% activity at 1 µM;
   - ceritinib has an IC50 of 13 µM;
   - TG101209, a fedratinib analogue, raised BUB1B protein 2.2× in CAKI-1
     cells but had an IC50 of 67 µM (Eur J Med Chem 2025, PMID 39818011).
     That suggests a ligand-stabilisation effect is possible in principle,
     and is worth noting for future screens.

**Implication for the method (scalability).** In loss-of-function diseases,
the "restore the protein" branch is usually a gap in target-based drug
databases. A pipeline that relies only on drug–target graphs will drift
toward downstream symptom pathways. Our pipeline keeps the mechanism branches
separate and adds mechanism-reasoned candidates explicitly, so it surfaces
that gap rather than hiding it.

Not carried forward:

- Venetoclax: senolysis in this setting needs BCL-xL, and venetoclax is not
  paediatric-approved for this use.
- Imatinib, bosutinib: weaker senolytic evidence than dasatinib.
- Anifrolumab: IV biologic; same issue as JAK inhibitors, with less
  paediatric experience.
- Interferons: wrong direction.

AVOID rows (tubulin agents) feed the oncology-interaction flag. They can't be
avoided in RMS care.
